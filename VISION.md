# Vision

Three White Lights exists to make a powerlifting career feel playable every day, with the weight, decision pressure, and earned satisfaction that make the real sport compelling.

The game brings casual players in through an immediate lifting mechanic and gives experienced lifters enough authenticity to care about the result. Every mode supports one persistent lifter, and the long arc pays off under the three lights on meet day.

## Who we serve

Three White Lights serves people who want a powerlifting game rather than a training spreadsheet with graphics. It should be approachable to someone who does not know the sport while remaining credible to the lifters who know when a total, attempt, or piece of equipment feels false.

When accessibility and authenticity appear to conflict, the game should teach or stage the real concept instead of replacing it with something arbitrary. Competitive lifters are the credibility bar, not the only audience.

## The outcome we create

A player should be able to open the game, make a meaningful training or lifting decision, feel the difference between an easy rep and a grind, and leave with visible progress in roughly a minute.

Over time, those short sessions build toward a career with qualifying totals, consequential attempt selection, rivals, gyms, and meets that feel earned. The emotional payoff is not merely a larger number. It is the tension before a third attempt and the relief of seeing three white lights.

## The responsibility we own

Three White Lights owns the translation of recognizable powerlifting into a compelling game loop. It must make strength, training judgment, meet structure, and progression understandable through play while preserving the parts of the sport that give decisions weight.

The game does not own real-world programming, injury prediction, medical guidance, or a literal simulation of physiology. It may abstract what makes the game playable, but it must not fake the public mathematics and meet rules that lifters can verify.

## The lift comes first

The moment of moving a heavy bar is the foundation. Timing, bar path, animation, sound, and haptics must make a maximal attempt feel heavier and more consequential than a routine rep.

No career system, idle economy, art volume, social layer, or monetization feature can compensate for an unsatisfying core lift. Work that does not improve or depend on that foundation waits until the relevant player test earns it.

## Authenticity is the moat

RPE relationships, estimated one-rep-max calculations, DOTS scoring, attempt order, judging, qualifying totals, and meet structure should use established sources and correct rules. The game may simplify how those ideas are introduced, but it should not invent authoritative-looking values.

Fatigue, setbacks, and other physiology are game-feel abstractions. They should behave plausibly and support decisions without claiming to model a real athlete's body.

## One lifter connects every mode

Career, daily simulation, arcade lifting, gym building, and meet day are not separate mini-games sharing a brand. They are different views of one lifter's progression.

A mode belongs when its rewards, choices, and consequences strengthen that shared career. A feature that creates an isolated economy or progression track must justify why it cannot support the central lifter instead.

## Daily play respects the player's time

The daily loop should reach meaningful input quickly and close with a clear sense of progress. It is a game habit, not a requirement to reproduce a full training session.

Streaks can reward consistency, but the game must not shame recovery or punish someone for having a life. Missed days, setbacks, and recovery mechanics should preserve agency and frame intelligent rest as part of progress.

## Progress is earned, never purchased

Money must not improve Total, estimated strength, training pace, meet performance, or competitive standing. Cosmetics, expression, and carefully bounded convenience can support the business only when they do not alter power.

Three White Lights rejects random paid recruitment, pay-to-win, forced advertising, and systems designed to convert frustration into spending. If advertising exists, it is optional and rewarded. If a player wins, the result should be attributable to play, decisions, and consistency.

## Meet day is the emotional centerpiece

Meet day should transform accumulated progress into tension. Attempt selection matters, misses have honest consequences, and a result feels legitimate enough that a powerlifter would recognize and share it.

Drama comes from pacing, scarcity, and consequence, not from constant interruption. High-intensity cut-ins, celebrations, and effects remain rare enough to retain meaning and remain skippable for daily players.

## The visual identity must communicate weight

The 16-bit visual direction should be readable at phone scale and detailed enough to show bar speed, sticking points, depth, plates, and strain. Retro style is a visual language, not permission for vague silhouettes or weightless animation.

Every visual flourish must support readability, intensity, or character. Expensive asset work waits when it does not help answer whether the core loop is fun.

## Human playtesting owns game feel

Agents, simulations, and automated tests can verify formulas, rules, state changes, and performance. They cannot establish that a lift feels satisfying in a person's hands.

Timing windows, animation curves, haptic patterns, and difficulty thresholds remain tunable. Claims about feel require real players, including both experienced lifters and newcomers. Unverified feel is reported as unverified, not passed by reasoning.

## Scope and non-goals

Three White Lights is a powerlifting career game with a short daily loop and meet-day payoff. It is not a workout tracker, coaching program, physiology simulator, casino, or generic idle game wearing powerlifting art.

Current systems and technology may change as prototypes answer their questions. The core lift, authentic public rules, one-lifter coherence, fair progression, daily respect, and meet-day tension remain the decision doctrine.

## Decision tests

A change fits this vision when it:

- Makes the lift feel heavier, clearer, or more satisfying.
- Deepens one persistent lifter's progression across modes.
- Preserves authentic powerlifting rules while teaching them through play.
- Strengthens the short daily loop or the earned tension of meet day.
- Supports sustainable revenue without selling competitive power.
- Can be validated at the appropriate bar, including human playtesting for feel.

We resist a change when it:

- Adds content or monetization before the core lifting loop earns expansion.
- Replaces recognizable powerlifting with arbitrary game logic where correctness is testable.
- Creates pay-to-win, random paid recruitment, forced ads, or punitive recovery mechanics.
- Makes a mode feel disconnected from the player's central lifter and career.
- Claims success in art, feel, or tension without inspecting the real artifact against a real reference or player response.
